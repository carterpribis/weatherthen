    About this site

    Search for a place, or click the map, to see the weather there on any day from 1836 to today. The report combines two kinds of source:

    • Weather models. From 1940 on, ERA5 (a reconstruction of the global weather by the European Centre for Medium-Range Weather Forecasts) gives conditions, highs and lows, rain, snow, sunshine and wind, hour by hour. For the last few months it uses recent weather-model data, so yesterday and last week are covered.
    • Station records. What thermometers and rain gauges at nearby weather stations actually recorded, from NOAA’s Global Historical Climatology Network. Some stations go back to the early 1800s.

    Up to 2015 the map shows the 20th Century Reanalysis (version 3), led by NOAA and the University of Colorado. It feeds historical surface-pressure readings from stations and ships into a modern weather model and rebuilds the atmosphere every 3 hours back to 1836. Colours are the day’s average temperature 2 metres above the ground. The lines are isobars: lines of equal air pressure at sea level, ringing lows (L), which bring storms, and highs (H), which bring settled weather. From 2016 on it shows ERA5, the European Centre for Medium-Range Weather Forecasts’ modern reanalysis, which uses millions of observations a day; new days appear about a week after they happen.

    The reconstruction is run 80 times with small differences. Where the runs agree, the map is well supported by observations; where they disagree, often over the southern oceans in the 1800s, it is an educated guess. Turn on Show uncertainty to see where. The base map (roads, towns, state and country borders) shows the world as it is today, for orientation; many borders and places were different back then.

    Privacy: place searches and the place and date you look up are sent to Open-Meteo and NOAA to fetch the weather. The site counts page views with Cloudflare Web Analytics, which uses no cookies and doesn’t track you across sites. Nothing else is collected.
